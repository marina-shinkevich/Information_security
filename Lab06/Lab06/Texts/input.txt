The city wakes up slowly on a Sunday morning. The streets are quiet, except for the occasional sound of a distant bus or a bird perched on a lamppost. The air smells of fresh bread from the bakery on the corner and wet asphalt from last night's rain.

I walk past small shops with closed shutters, their colorful graffiti telling stories of the night before. A cat stretches lazily by a doorstep, watching me with half-closed eyes. Somewhere, a coffee machine hisses, and the first customers of the day step out holding warm cups, their breath mixing with the steam.

By the time the sun climbs over the rooftops, the city stirs again. Cars appear, voices multiply, and the silence gives way to the familiar hum of daily life. For just a moment, though, everything felt calm — as if the world had pressed pause before starting another busy week.
